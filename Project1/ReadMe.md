# Educational Level Distribution Across Four Countries

This analysis compares the educational qualifications of respondents
from Argentina, Brazil, Nigeria, and South Korea. The categories
examined are bachelor's degree, doctoral degree, master's degree, and
some college or university education.

## Argentina

The educational distribution in Argentina shows that respondents with a
**master's degree** recorded the highest frequency, followed by those
with a **bachelor's degree**. Respondents who had attended some college
or university without earning a bachelor's degree had a lower
representation, while **doctoral degree holders** recorded the lowest
frequency.

This indicates that master's degree holders made up the largest
educational group among the respondents in Argentina.

## Brazil

A similar pattern can be observed in Brazil. Respondents with a
**master's degree** recorded the highest frequency, followed by
**bachelor's degree** holders and then **doctoral degree** holders.
Respondents with some college or university education had the lowest
representation.

## Nigeria

In Nigeria, **bachelor's degree** holders recorded the highest
frequency, followed by respondents with a **master's degree**. Those
with some college or university education had a lower representation,
while **doctoral degree** holders recorded the lowest frequency.

## South Korea

In South Korea, respondents with a **master's degree** recorded the
highest frequency, followed by **bachelor's degree** holders and
**doctoral degree** holders. Respondents with some college or university
education had the lowest frequency.

## Overall Comparison

Overall, the comparison of educational levels across the four countries
reveals noticeable differences in the distribution of academic
qualifications among respondents. Nigeria recorded the highest frequency
of bachelor's degree holders, while Brazil had the highest frequency of
doctoral degree holders and respondents with some college or university
education. For master's degree holders, Brazil and Nigeria recorded
relatively similar frequencies, with Brazil slightly higher. Argentina
and South Korea generally recorded lower frequencies across the
educational categories compared to Brazil and Nigeria.

This comparison shows that bachelor's and master's degree holders were
the most represented educational groups across the four countries, while
doctoral degree holders and respondents with some college or university
education had comparatively lower frequencies.
